# M0C failure ledger

Qualification failures and independent-review findings, in order of discovery, with
its classification, evidence and disposition. Superseded runs are kept under
`attempts/`, each with an `ATTEMPT-NOTE.md` giving its build and diagnosis.
Nothing was overwritten to make the final runs look clean.

Classes are the M0B failure-policy categories: implementation defect,
permissions/config issue, provider limitation, macOS limitation, SPEC
assumption failure. Harness defects are listed separately: they changed what
a run could prove, not what the product does.

Builds: `26ce728` and `a6ef67a` (pre-checks), `a983837` (smoke), `5a6149a`,
then the official `8c82212`. Harness fixes after `8c82212` (`1b46091`,
`89d8572`, `df4c75e`) touch only `tests/native`; the installed application is unchanged. Independent review of `6f903c2` reopens C-02 and adds H-10–H-12 below; raw run artifacts are unchanged.

## C-02 independent-review reproduction and closure

The startup bypass is visible without inventing a hostile environment. In `apps/agent-macos/core/src/lib.rs`, `XPC_SERVICE_NAME` classifies the observed notification LaunchServices job as unsupervised, but startup still reads `journal.observation_enabled()` and stores it in `RUNTIME`. `Runtime::admission_open()` in `state.rs` checks enabled/maintenance/sleep only; discovery and capture use that gate. The `writer.rs` supervision refusal protects only a later explicit enable command. Missing or unrecognized service names also default to supervised rather than providing positive ownership proof.

With the saved preference enabled and the system background item unavailable after the previous companion exits, clicking an existing notification can therefore start a control app that immediately becomes the enabled observer. The same instance can win the writer lock during a notification/crash-relaunch race. This violates SPEC §18.9's unavailable-service behavior and independent supervision; it does not bypass the exclusive lock or demonstrate lost journal state.

Keep the persisted preference separate from effective admission, require positively verified login-item/service ownership at startup and enable, and preserve the inspector/notification intent through control-only handoff without depending on another enable command. Treat unknown supervision conservatively. Native closure requires unavailable service plus enabled preference, an enabled crash/notification race followed by another no-UI/no-hook crash relaunch, and the original stop → notification → enable sequence. Verify one login-item-owned writer, retained intents and preserved disabled/enabled preferences throughout.
