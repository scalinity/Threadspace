## Evidence

## Decisions

1. **Reduction is over evidence sets.** A fact adds evidence to the records it references (a turn's outcome set, an execution's end reasons, a wait scope's positive witnesses, clear barriers and owner decisions, an item's resolution causes); every displayed state is re-derived from that evidence in a fixed dependency order. No field is "whatever arrived last" where arrival order could differ between valid deliveries, except the three scoped in §10. This is how valid permutations converge (INV-14) and why a terminal turn, an ENDED execution and a lost binding cannot regress. A session with no active turn shows its causally latest settled turn's state; settled turns that cannot be ordered decide by severity. The public read model shows exactly this state: a session view takes its turn state, execution presence and observation from the materialized canonical session, never from turn or activation arrival order.
2. **Identity allocation is an admission step; derived records are name-based.** Admission allocates a random UUID only for a never-seen native key, recorded in `identity_assignments` with the cursor that allocated it. Records the reducer derives (attention items, wait episodes, outbox intents) get RFC 9562 version-8 UUIDs from SHA-256 of their canonical scope, looked up by scope first, so exact replay needs no randomness and a migrated M0 item keeps its ID.
3. **Semantic equality excludes presentation and delivery history, never owner decisions.** Beyond SPEC §5.5's list (allocated UUIDs, cursors and revisions, receipt and creation times, allocation-order metadata such as activation numbers), the comparison excludes display names, summaries, route records and surface status (presentation or diagnostics), and compares the outbox by its currently eligible intents: whether a suppressed intent was ever submitted depends on when resolving evidence arrived (SPEC §7.6 accepts that a banner submitted before late evidence may have appeared). It omits a wait item whose episode a late clear emptied (§4), which holds no evidence and no owner state. It includes every wait scope's owner decisions by the evidence each covered (its native causal points and the count of positives without one) and the episodes whose items it applies to now, names waits by their turn, and compares a command on a wait item as that decision. Decisions made on different evidence therefore differ even when they act on the same episode: histories in which the owner was shown different evidence are different histories, and only equal coverage converges. The session's turn state is compared.
4. **Aggregate waits partition by comparable clears; owner decisions follow their evidence.** A wait with known turn identity belongs to that turn's scope; one without (an inventory wait) is session-scoped and never guessed onto a turn. A positive witness belongs to the episode numbered by the comparable clears before it; it is cleared by a comparable clear at or after it. A positive incomparable with a clear keeps its episode uncertain and unresolved. Native wait resolution is re-derived from current evidence (so late incomparable evidence can withhold it). An owner's Acknowledge, Resolve or Snooze on a wait item is kept on the scope with the evidence the owner was shown: the positive witnesses of the episode acted on and how many positives without a causal point it held (those have no identity, and their count only grows, so a decision covers the first that many). A decision applies only to the evidence it covered, and its record never changes when the partition does. An episode's active evidence is its positives no comparable clear ended (open or uncertain) and its positives without a causal point. Its item takes an action only when that action's decisions together cover every active witness, so evidence the owner never handled keeps it actionable: a new wait after a clear, a positive that joins the episode after the decision, or one a late clear merges into a decided episode. A repartition therefore never transfers a decision to evidence it did not cover, while a late, earlier clear that moves P10 into the next episode carries P10's decision with it. Once all of an episode's evidence has ended natively, every decision made on it stays on its item as history. The emptied episode's item is resolved as superseded (lifted if the episode reappears). An intent the reducer suppressed before submission re-arms when its item becomes eligible again; submitted intents and suppressed migrated M0 intents are history.
5. **In-place `exec` invalidates relative to the proof.** A process keeps every observed image with its causal point, and a binding records the causal point of its proof. It is invalidated as EXECUTABLE_REPLACED when the current image (the observation every other precedes) is determinable and differs from the image it was proven with, or once an image other than the proven one is observed causally after its proof. The second rule only grows with evidence, so no later observation revives a lost proof, while a fresh proof made after the process returned to the same executable (A → B → A) is valid. A proof without a causal point counts any observation of its image that another follows. Otherwise unordered observations assume no current image and leave invalidation to explicit ends, as the M0B store did.
6. **M0 projections are promoted, not paralleled.** The M0 tables are the reducer's materialized projections (same columns plus new ones); a store written before M1 gets a deterministic `M0_BASELINE` checkpoint built from its rows, an intent the M0 store recorded keeps its request ID (the reducer finds intents by their item), and an owner command the M0 store committed stays idempotent: a retried original request is recognised by M0's own fingerprint of it and returns the original receipt. Migration 3 rebuilds `turns` so native turn uniqueness includes the actor, as the turn key does. A store with a newer schema or reducer checkpoint is refused by a read-only preflight before any pragma, migration or bootstrap write, creating nothing in the store directory: a `-wal` is always read (never through `immutable`, which ignores it), beside a `-shm` with `readonly_shm`, and with no `-shm` from a private temporary copy of the main file and `-wal`, where the `-shm` SQLite needs is created instead. The one accepted exception is a store left with a hot rollback journal: the preflight opens it writable and SQLite rolls the journal back, restoring the last committed bytes. That is a chosen trade-off, not a necessity; inspecting a private copy would refuse such a store without the rollback. A checkpoint written by an earlier reducer is upgraded when the store opens, to the same result wherever that checkpoint sits. Its JSON is read through that version's representation (reducer 2 counts a decision's covered positives without a causal point where reducer 1 kept a flag, read as 1). The journal entries after it were admitted by that reducer, which committed their notification work with them, so they are replayed only to recover the state: as catch-up, never as live work, with the owner decisions they record read as that reducer recorded them. The recovered state is rebased onto the rows that reducer committed: each notification intent takes its committed state, detail, times and revision, an intent it never committed is created by the upgrade, and an attention item takes its committed resolution time (NULL as none; any other type refuses the upgrade before it writes) and, when its row changes, the upgrade's cursor as its revision; the re-derivation keeps that time only while the item's evidence still resolves it. Every record is then re-derived under the current rules, and the result is materialized and checkpointed in one transaction. A committed PENDING intent stays PENDING, a suppressed one whose item is eligible again re-arms HELD, an intent the upgrade creates is HELD, and the upgrade submits nothing. Every projection row has one builder, used to write it and to hash it, so `tables == state` is checkable at any time.
7. **Claude conventional hooks map conservatively (CLAUDE_CLASSIC_LIMITED).** Identity, unaccepted submissions, Stop/SubagentStop as response boundaries, tool phases and subordinate identification; never a turn outcome or execution end (conventional hooks carry no native turn identity). SessionEnd stays identity-only until M2/M3 match activations.
8. **Two deliveries are deliberately later milestones.** Canonical attention creates durable outbox intents in the same transaction, but OS submission of those intents is M5's notification product; Claude inventory `waiting`/`waitingFor` remain display metadata until M2's inventory integration maps them to session-scoped wait episodes.
9. **Capture budgets and spool bounds are enforced structurally.** The hook exits 0 silently on every path with a watchdog at the 250 ms wall budget. Every hook is its own process, so a spool publication decides its bounds and renames into `ready/` under one advisory quota lock (`quota.lock`, `flock`), released by the kernel if its holder dies and waited for at most 50 ms; a publisher that cannot take it in time records a `spoolbusy` loss marker instead. The 100,000-record bound is counted from the listing and the 256 MiB bound from real file sizes. A batch the store refuses (a constraint or an invalid record) is re-admitted one record at a time, and a lone refused record is answered `NOT_ACCEPTED` and quarantined, so one record cannot stall the drain. Spool loss is journaled before its markers and expired records leave the spool. Observation IDs are accepted only in canonical lowercase form, the form receipts name.

10. **Three fields stay last-observation until M2.** An execution's attach mode and presence, the human follow-up frontier (an acceptance followed by a rejection of the same input), and a session's observer link state take the latest observation. Today no valid delivery order differs: reconciliation sends one attach per execution, nothing rejects an input it accepted, and only the synthetic provider reports a link state. M2 adds producers of repeated attaches, rejections and link changes, and its exit requires each to be an evidence set first (MILESTONES M2).

## Consequences

- SPEC/MILESTONES describe these as the current design; a later reducer version must migrate the checkpoint representation (SPEC §9.3).
- M2 owns mod-based native turn identity and outcomes, inventory wait mapping, the native Mark handled control and the three evidence sets of §10; M5 owns OS delivery of canonical outbox intents.
